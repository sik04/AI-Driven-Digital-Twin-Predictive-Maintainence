6. Proposed Methodology

6.1 Methodological Framework and Algorithmic Overview

The proposed IntelliTwin methodology establishes an integrated, mathematically grounded pipeline that transforms high-frequency multivariate telemetry from degrading industrial rotational machinery into calibrated, risk-aware sequential maintenance decisions [1]–[3]. Existing predictive maintenance approaches predominantly exhibit severe methodological fragmentation: machine learning models optimize symmetric statistical loss functions (e.g., Mean Squared Error) decoupled from operational cost penalties [1], [4]; conformal prediction algorithms evaluate marginal coverage in isolation without adapting to regime-induced non-exchangeability [3], [9]; and maintenance decision models rely on parametric distributional assumptions that fail under non-linear wear dynamics [5], [6].

To resolve these limitations, the IntelliTwin framework formulates a unified seven-stage methodological pipeline governed by rigorous data-leakage controls:

  1. Causal Telemetry Ingestion and Multi-Regime Normalization: Standardizing continuous multivariate sensor streams from NASA C-MAPSS under strict 30-cycle causal lookback windowing (W = 30) and training-only Z-score normalization [1].
  2. Dual Base Prognostic Estimation: Generating continuous point forecasts ŷi(t) of Remaining Useful Life via complementary shallow gradient-boosted decision trees (HistGradientBoostingRegressor) and deep recurrent neural networks (PyTorch LSTM) [3], [4].
  3. Engine-Weighted Asymmetric Conformal Calibration: Inverting nonconformity scores weighted at the engine level to construct finite-sample prediction intervals C1-α(Xi(t)) = [Li(t), Ui(t)] targeting nominal 90% coverage (1 - α = 0.90) [7], [9].
  4. Condition-Adaptive Regime and Wear-Stage Partitioning: Structuring calibration bins across discrete operational flight regimes (k = 6) and piece-wise linear wear stages (t → Ti) to preserve conditional reliability under operational drift [4], [10].
  5. Discrete-Event Sequential Maintenance Utility Simulation: Evaluating dynamic stopping times τi governed by lower prediction bounds Li(t) under asymmetric operational economics (CFAIL ≫ CPM) formalized in Standardized Cost Units (SCU) [5], [8].
  6. Matched-Conservatism Attribution Auditing: Contrasting policy outcomes against widened-interval, safety-margin, and early-intervention control families to verify genuine decision-relevant uncertainty value [8], [12].
  7. Digital Twin Replay Synchronization: Mirroring asset degradation in real time via an offline trace-driven replay engine (TwinReplayEngine) with strict numerical verification parity (atol ≤ 10-4) linked to a FastAPI REST service and operator dashboard [2], [13], [14].

6.2 Telemetry Ingestion, Anti-Leakage Windowing and Normalization

The experimental data foundation is established using the Commercial Modular Aero-Propulsion System Simulation (C-MAPSS) benchmark corpus developed by NASA Ames Research Center [1]. To rigorously investigate multi-regime degradation dynamics, single-condition baselines, and cross-fault generalizability, three distinct sub-corpora are utilized:

  • FD002 (Primary Multi-Regime Benchmark): 260 training engines and 259 test engines operating across six discrete environmental regimes (altitude 0–42,000 ft, Mach number 0.0–0.84, throttle resolver angle 20–100) subject to high-pressure compressor (HPC) degradation.
  • FD001 (Single-Condition Control Benchmark): 100 training engines and 100 test engines operating under sea-level static flight conditions with single-mode HPC degradation, providing a stationary reference baseline.
  • FD004 (Multi-Regime / Multi-Fault Robustness Benchmark): 249 training engines and 248 test engines operating across six flight conditions with combined HPC and fan degradation modes.

Causal Sliding Window Formulation

At each discrete operating cycle t ≥ W, historical multivariate sensor telemetry is segmented into a causal sliding lookback window with fixed temporal horizon W = 30 cycles:

Xi(t) = [xi(t - W + 1), xi(t - W + 2), ..., xi(t)]T ∈ ℝW × d

where xi(τ) ∈ ℝd denotes the sensory observation vector recorded for engine i at operating cycle τ. Causal windowing enforces that predictions at cycle t depend strictly on historical observations τ ≤ t, eliminating prospective temporal data leakage. During the initial warm-up phase (1 ≤ t < W), prognostic inference is deferred until a complete 30-cycle observation history is accumulated.

Feature Selection and Sensor Pruning

The raw C-MAPSS telemetry schema contains 26 columns: engine identifier, cycle index, three operational flight settings, and 21 thermo-mechanical degradation sensors. An automated variance and rank-correlation audit across all datasets revealed six completely invariant or uninformative channels exhibiting zero variance or uninformative responses:

Sdropped = { s1 (T2), s5 (P2), s6 (P15), s10 (epr), s16 (farB), s19 (Nf_dmd) }

These six invariant channels are pruned to prevent covariance rank deficiency and numerical instability during gradient backpropagation. The retained feature space comprises d = 17 dimensions: three operational flight condition settings (z1, z2, z3) and 14 informative degradation sensors (total temperatures at LPC/HPC/LPT outlets, total pressures, physical and corrected core/fan rotational speeds, static pressure, fuel flow ratio, coolant bleed rates).

Anti-Leakage Normalization and Partitioning

To prevent data snooping across experimental splits, Z-score standardization parameters (mean μtrain and standard deviation σtrain) are computed exclusively on the designated training split:

X̃i(t) = (Xi(t) - μtrain) / (σtrain + ε)

where ε = 10-8 prevents division by zero. The exact training parameters (μtrain, σtrain) are subsequently applied immutably to normalize calibration, validation, and held-out test splits. Partitioning is strictly enforced at the engine level: all time-series observations originating from a given physical engine i belong exclusively to one split (Train, Calibration, Validation, or Test), ensuring zero inter-engine correlation leakage.

6.3 Base Prognostic Regression Models

Prognostic regression estimates the continuous Remaining Useful Life yi(t) = Ti - t directly from the standardized window X̃i(t), where Ti denotes the total failure cycle of engine i. In accordance with standard aerospace reliability engineering [1], RUL targets are capped at an upper piece-wise linear threshold Rmax = 125 cycles:

yi*(t) = min(Rmax, Ti - t)

reflecting the empirical physical reality that initial operational wear does not exhibit measurable degradation signatures during early engine life.

To evaluate algorithmic robustness across diverse learning paradigms, two complementary base model families are constructed and frozen:

1. HistGradientBoostingRegressor (GBR)

The GBR base model represents high-capacity tree-based ensemble learning. The temporal window X̃i(t) ∈ ℝ30 × 17 is flattened into a 510-dimensional vector vi(t) = vec(X̃i(t)). Multi-quantile gradient boosting optimizes the asymmetric pinball loss function:

Lρ(y, ŷ) = max(ρ(y - ŷ), (1 - ρ)(ŷ - y))

trained across quantiles ρ ∈ {0.05, 0.50, 0.95} with histogram-binned split finding (max bins = 255, learning rate = 0.05, max depth = 8, L2 regularization = 1.0).

2. PyTorch Deep Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM)

The LSTM base model captures temporal dependencies directly from sequential tensor representations. The architecture comprises:

  • Input recurrent layer processing sequential vectors xi(τ) ∈ ℝ17 over sequence length W = 30.
  • Two stacked bidirectional LSTM layers with hidden dimension h = 64 (128 units bidirectional) incorporating variational dropout (p = 0.20) between recurrent layers to mitigate co-adaptation.
  • Fully connected projection head with non-linear LeakyReLU activation (α = 0.01), linear projection, and a scalar output neuron forecasting continuous point predictions ŷi(t).
  • Optimization using AdamW (β1 = 0.9, β2 = 0.999, weight decay = 10-4, initial learning rate η = 10-3) under Mean Squared Error loss with cosine annealing learning rate scheduling.

A complete 30-model benchmark matrix (3 datasets × 2 architectures × 5 seeds [2026, 2027, 2028, 2029, 2030]) is trained and frozen under Architecture Decision Record ADR-018.

6.4 Condition-Adaptive Hierarchical Conformal Calibration

To provide finite-sample, distribution-free prediction intervals with mathematically guaranteed coverage, the IntelliTwin framework formulates an engine-weighted, condition-adaptive Inductive Conformal Prediction (ICP) calibration methodology [4], [7], [9].

Asymmetric Decision-Aware Nonconformity Scoring

Standard conformal prediction utilizes symmetric absolute residuals si(t) = |yi(t) - ŷi(t)|, which treat overestimation and underestimation errors identically [4]. In safety-critical aerospace prognostics, overestimation errors (ŷ > y) risk catastrophic in-flight failure, whereas underestimation errors (ŷ < y) result merely in premature overhaul [1]. To reflect this operational asymmetry, nonconformity scoring incorporates dynamic shape factors parameterized by scaling exponent γ ∈ {0, 1, 2} and asymmetry coefficient β ∈ ℝ:

σ(p̂) = (1 + p̂ / 125.0)γ,      z(p̂) = exp(-p̂ / 20.0)
a(p̂) = σ(p̂) · exp(β · z(p̂)),      b(p̂) = σ(p̂) · exp(-β · z(p̂))

The decision-aware nonconformity score function is then formulated as:

si(t) = max( (p̂i(t) - yi(t)) / a(p̂i(t)), (yi(t) - p̂i(t)) / b(p̂i(t)), 0 )

where p̂i(t) = ŷi(t) represents the base point prediction. When γ = 0 and β = 0, the shape factors reduce to a(p̂) = 1 and b(p̂) = 1, recovering the classical symmetric absolute residual score si(t) = |yi(t) - p̂i(t)|.

Engine-Weighted Hierarchical Empirical Quantiles

Because run-to-failure engine telemetry contains multiple observations per physical asset, pooling all cycle observations equally violates the foundational exchangeability assumption of conformal prediction due to strong temporal autocorrelation and unequal engine lifespans [9]. To address this clustered data structure without discarding observations, calibration quantile estimation applies exact engine-level observation weighting.

Let Dcal denote the calibration split containing m distinct physical engines, where engine i contributes ni valid temporal windows. Each individual observation from engine i is assigned weight wi,t = 1 / (m · ni), guaranteeing that every physical engine exerts identical aggregate influence ∑t wi,t = 1 / m. The engine-weighted empirical cumulative distribution function is defined as:

Fdev(q) = (1 / m) ∑i=1m [ (1 / ni) ∑t=1ni 𝕀(si,t ≤ q) ]

For a target nominal coverage level 1 - α = 0.90, the conformal quantile threshold q̂1-α is defined as the smallest observed calibration score satisfying:

q̂1-α = inf { q ∈ ℝ : Fdev(q) ≥ ⌈(m + 1)(1 - α)⌉ / m }

which provides finite-sample coverage validity on held-out test engines under cluster exchangeability [9].

Condition-Adaptive Regime Partitioning

To prevent localized undercoverage during high-stress operating regimes (e.g., high altitude and high Mach number in FD002), operational conditions are clustered into K = 6 distinct regimes using K-means clustering on flight settings (z1, z2, z3). Conformal thresholds q̂1-α(k) are calibrated conditionally within each regime cluster k ∈ {1, ..., K}, ensuring that high-stress operational profiles receive appropriately calibrated uncertainty bands [4], [10].

Interval Inversion

Given the calibrated threshold q̂1-α, the finite-sample prediction interval C1-α(Xi(t)) = [Li(t), Ui(t)] is computed via exact closed-form score inversion:

Li(t) = max(0, p̂i(t) - q̂1-α · a(p̂i(t))),      Ui(t) = p̂i(t) + q̂1-α · b(p̂i(t))

where Li(t) represents the conservative lower bound on Remaining Useful Life utilized for risk-directed maintenance decisions.

6.5 Discrete-Event Sequential Maintenance Utility Simulation

Operational value is realized exclusively through the maintenance intervention decisions guided by prognostic intervals [5], [8]. IntelliTwin models sequential asset health monitoring via a cycle-by-cycle discrete-event maintenance simulator.

Sequential Decision Rule

At each flight cycle t ≥ W, the Digital Twin evaluates the lower conformal bound Li(t). When the conservative lower bound drops below a prespecified operational intervention threshold θ (with default θ = 0), a preventive maintenance (PM) order is dispatched:

τi = inf { t ≥ W : Li(t) ≤ θ }

where τi denotes the sequential stopping time for physical engine i.

Standardized Cost Units (SCU) Lifecycle Evaluation

If engine retirement occurs prior to catastrophic mechanical failure (τi < Ti), planned maintenance is successfully executed. If the engine reaches its terminal cycle before maintenance is triggered (τi ≥ Ti), an unscheduled in-service failure occurs at cycle Ti. To quantify lifecycle economic consequences independent of fluctuating currency values, costs are evaluated in Standardized Cost Units (SCU) under the reference scenario:

Costi(τi) = CPM + CWASTE · (Ti - τi)    if τi < Ti (Successful Preventive Maintenance)
Costi(τi) = CFAIL                                             if τi ≥ Ti (Unscheduled In-Service Failure)

with standardized economic weights:

  • CPM = 1.0 SCU: Cost of a planned, scheduled hangar overhaul.
  • CFAIL = 5.0 SCU: Severe penalty of an uncontained in-service engine failure (including emergency diversion, secondary damage, and operational disruption).
  • CWASTE = 0.20 SCU per cycle: Cost penalty for usable component life discarded by premature early replacement (Ti - τi).

Total fleet maintenance cost is computed as the average lifecycle expenditure across all evaluated engines: Cost̄ = (1 / N) ∑i=1N Costi(τi).

6.6 Controlled Attribution Protocol (Matched-Conservatism Baselines)

A critical methodological vulnerability in prognostic literature is the failure to disambiguate genuine uncertainty calibration from trivial over-conservatism [8]. An uncalibrated heuristic that arbitrarily replaces engines 30 cycles early will trivially eliminate in-service failures (CFAIL), but does so by squandering massive usable life (CWASTE) [5], [8].

To address Research Question 3 (RQ3), IntelliTwin benchmarks the proposed decision-aware method against three prespecified matched-conservatism control families:

  1. Family 1: Uniformly Widened Conformal Intervals (δ-Widened): Inflates the global conformal threshold by fixed scalar constants q̂' = q̂ + δ, testing whether improvements stem merely from wider coverage bands.
  2. Family 2: Point-Prediction Safety Margins (Δ-Margin): Implements deterministic safety offsets p̂'(t) = p̂(t) - Δ, triggering maintenance when p̂'(t) ≤ θ without interval calibration.
  3. Family 3: Earlier Scheduled Intervention Thresholds (θ-Offset): Advances maintenance triggers by raising the intervention threshold θ' = θ + τadvance, evaluating whether simple early scheduling reproduces the proposed method's cost savings.

A positive decision attribution is established only if the proposed method achieves statistically significant lifecycle cost savings (Δcost > 0.20 SCU per engine) against every matched-conservatism control family [12].

6.7 Statistical Inference Protocol and Decision Rules

To ensure rigorous hypothesis testing without distributional assumptions, statistical inference is conducted using engine-level cluster bootstrap with 10,000 resamples [12].

Family-Wise Error Rate Control

Across the four formal research questions (RQ1–RQ4), family-wise type I error is strictly controlled at αfamily = 0.05 using the Bonferroni correction:

αtest = 0.05 / 4 = 0.0125

All decision intervals are evaluated as 98.75% two-sided percentile bootstrap intervals ([Q0.00625, Q0.99375]).

Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) for Robustness

For Research Question 4 (RQ4), cross-condition robustness across the six evaluation cells (FD001, FD002, FD004 × GBR, LSTM) is audited against pre-registered non-harm equivalence bounds [12]:

Lower 98.75% CI of Δcost ≥ -0.20 SCU,      Lower 98.75% CI of Δerror ≥ -0.01

guaranteeing that condition-adaptive calibration does not induce material operational degradation in any individual configuration.

6.8 Digital Twin Synchronization Replay Prototype

To ground the algorithmic formulation in operational engineering reality, IntelliTwin implements a real-time trace-driven Digital Twin synchronization engine (TwinReplayEngine) [2], [13], [14].

Trace-Driven State Synchronization

The replay engine ingests pre-recorded run-to-failure sensor matrices and emits discrete telemetry frames cycle-by-cycle. State synchronization adheres to ISO 23247 digital twin principles [14], maintaining:

  • Telemetry sliding buffer maintaining the active 30-cycle causal lookback window Xi(t).
  • Feature scaling layer applying training Z-scores X̃i(t) in real time.
  • Inference executor generating dual RUL point predictions ŷGBR(t) and ŷLSTM(t).
  • Conformal calibration filter generating lower and upper bounds [Li(t), Ui(t)].
  • Dynamic health index estimator HIi(t) = min(1.0, p̂i(t) / Rmax) reflecting remaining operational integrity.
  • Decision trigger monitor assessing intervention rules 𝕀(Li(t) ≤ θ) and dispatching warning states (NOMINAL, ADVISORY, CRITICAL_INTERVENTION).

Numerical Parity Verification

To guarantee that real-time replay does not introduce numerical artifacts or windowing drift, the replay engine is audited against offline batch execution:

maxt | p̂stream(t) - p̂batch(t) | ≤ 10-4,      maxt | Lstream(t) - Lbatch(t) | ≤ 10-4

Parity is verified across all benchmark datasets via 37 automated pytest modules, establishing that the interactive Digital Twin operator dashboard at port 8000 displays mathematically identical outputs to the scientific evaluation pipeline.
