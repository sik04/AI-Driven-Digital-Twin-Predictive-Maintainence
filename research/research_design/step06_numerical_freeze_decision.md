### Rationale and Principles
- $C_{\text{PM}} = 1.0$ defines the normalized simulation cost unit (SCU).
- In `primary_reference`, functional failure is penalized as five PM units ($C_{\text{FAIL}} = 5.0$), and each discarded usable cycle costs 0.20 SCU ($C_{\text{WASTE}} = 0.20$).
- The two secondary scenarios independently double the failure cost ($C_{\text{FAIL}} = 10.0$) or discarded-life cost ($C_{\text{WASTE}} = 0.40$) while holding other coefficients fixed.
- Approval of sensitivity scenarios permits prespecified secondary sensitivity analyses; it does not promote them to primary confirmatory outcomes. No sensitivity scenario may replace an unfavorable primary result.
- These are transparent benchmark preferences chosen before confirmatory results, not estimated airline costs or universal engineering constants.

## 3. Cost Model & Equality Horizon Interpretation

For all scenarios, maintenance costs are calculated strictly as:
- **Preventive Maintenance Cost**:
  $$\text{Cost}_{\text{PM}} = C_{\text{PM}} + C_{\text{WASTE}} \times W$$
  where $W$ is the uncapped wasted remaining useful life ($W = T - \tau_{\text{decision}} \ge 1$ cycle for interventions at $\tau_{\text{decision}} \le T-1$).
- **Functional Failure Cost**:
  $$\text{Cost}_{\text{FAIL}} = C_{\text{FAIL}}$$

### Equality Horizon ($r_{\text{equal}}$)
For positive discarded-life costs ($C_{\text{WASTE}} > 0$), the breakeven remaining life where PM cost equals failure cost is:
$$r_{\text{equal}} = \frac{C_{\text{FAIL}} - C_{\text{PM}}}{C_{\text{WASTE}}}$$

The three $r_{\text{equal}}$ values are:
- **`primary_reference`**: $(5.0 - 1.0) / 0.20 = 20$ cycles.
- **`failure_sensitive`**: $(10.0 - 1.0) / 0.20 = 45$ cycles.
- **`waste_sensitive`**: $(5.0 - 1.0) / 0.40 = 10$ cycles.

**Critical Interpretive Boundaries**:
- These values identify exact equality between maintaining immediately and accepting unmitigated failure. They are **NOT** optimal replacement times, policy thresholds, warning horizons, or evidence of a scientifically optimal cost ratio.
- At equality ($W = r_{\text{equal}}$), the costs are identical; preventive maintenance is not strictly cheaper.
- Interventions with $W > r_{\text{equal}}$ represent *cost-inefficient early PM* under that scenario.

### Theoretical Oracle Benchmark
Under these frozen positive-waste-cost scenarios, an idealized oracle with perfect knowledge of failure cycle $T$ and zero intervention lead time would intervene at cycle $T-1$ ($W = 1$ cycle):
- **`primary_reference`**: $\text{Cost} = 1.0 + 0.20 \times 1 = 1.20$ SCU.
- **`failure_sensitive`**: $\text{Cost} = 1.0 + 0.20 \times 1 = 1.20$ SCU.
- **`waste_sensitive`**: $\text{Cost} = 1.0 + 0.40 \times 1 = 1.40$ SCU.

*Notice: This oracle benchmark serves solely as an analytical reference. Oracle failure knowledge must never be provided to a deployable predictive policy.*

## 5. Numerical Evaluation Contract Summary

The authoritative numerical thresholds are frozen under `step06-v2` in `research/hypotheses/hypothesis_framework.yaml`:
- **Primary Nominal Coverage**: 90% ($\alpha = 0.10$). Secondary sensitivity levels at 80% and 95%.
- **Cost Improvement Margin**: $\Delta_{\text{cost}} > 0.20$ SCU per engine.
- **Coverage Error Improvement Margin**: $\Delta_{\text{error}} > 0.01$ (one percentage point).
- **Coverage Error Ceiling**: Maximum absolute error $E < 0.05$ (85%–95% empirical acceptance band).
- **Interval Noninferiority Margins**: $D_{\text{width}} < 1.0$ cycle for MPIW; $D_{\text{score}} < 1.0$ cycle for Winkler interval score.
- **Robustness Non-Harm Margins**: $\Delta_{\text{cost}} > -0.20$ SCU per engine; $\Delta_{\text{error}} > -0.01$.
- **Observed Failure Safeguard**: Fleet failure rate increase $\le 0.0$.
- **Statistical Inference**: Stratified cluster bootstrap ($B = 10,000$), 98.75% decision intervals (Bonferroni across RQ1–RQ4), intersection-union decision principle, missing-group conservative bound propagation.
