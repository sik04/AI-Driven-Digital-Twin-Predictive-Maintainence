\section*{Governance Rules on Success Criteria and Protocol Integrity}

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Success Criteria Rule:} We discard old arbitrary thresholds (5\%, 10\%, 15\%, 75\%) unless independently justified. Each RQ defines a pre-specified primary outcome, comparator set, practical effect threshold policy, and statistical decision rule before confirmatory testing.

\textbf{Outcome Hierarchy Rule:} The primary outcome determines the main conclusion for each research question. Secondary/supporting outcomes verify constraints, characterize trade-offs, and provide interpretation, but a favorable secondary outcome cannot replace failure on the primary outcome.

\textbf{No Post-Hoc Modification Rule:} No post-hoc modification of confirmatory evaluation criteria is permitted. Primary metrics, comparator sets, subgroup definitions, practical-effect thresholds, statistical decision rules, and outcome hierarchies must not be altered after observing confirmatory results. Any late modification must be documented as a protocol deviation and labelled exploratory.
\end{quote}

\section*{Statistical Uncertainty Framework}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Independent Unit}: The engine unit is the independent statistical sample unit. Individual sequence time windows or time steps extracted from the same physical engine exhibit strong temporal autocorrelation and must \textbf{NEVER} be treated as independent statistical observations.
  \item \textbf{Paired Comparisons}: Comparisons between the proposed method and baseline models evaluated on the same engine units or dataset splits must be paired.
  \item \textbf{Confidence Intervals}: For each primary metric, statistical uncertainty will be quantified using a \textbf{95\% engine-level paired/cluster bootstrap confidence interval} (resampling engine units intact).
  \item \textbf{Effect Reporting}: Effect sizes and their corresponding 95\% engine-level confidence intervals must always be reported together.
  \item \textbf{Secondary P-Values}: P-values or statistical hypothesis test results, if reported, are secondary supporting evidence and cannot independently determine whether a research question is supported.
  \item \textbf{Deferred Statistical Parameterizations}: Exact bootstrap resample counts, exact CI construction variants (percentile vs.\ BCa), secondary significance tests, and multiplicity corrections remain deferred to the detailed experimental protocol.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Global Decision Rule and Threshold Governance}

\begin{quote}
After practical-effect thresholds are finalized, an RQ will be considered \textbf{supported} only when the estimated effect and its uncertainty provide evidence of a practically meaningful benefit; \textbf{unsupported} when the evidence indicates no meaningful benefit, an adverse effect, or violation of a required reliability constraint; and \textbf{inconclusive} when the confidence interval or cross-comparator evidence does not allow a clear distinction between meaningful and negligible effects.

Exact numerical practical-effect thresholds remain deferred until maintenance-simulator validation and must be frozen before confirmatory testing. Confirmatory results cannot be used to select, tune, relax, or redefine these thresholds.
\end{quote}

\section*{Research Problem Statement}

\begin{quote}
Existing RUL research has separately studied conformal uncertainty calibration, condition/regime-aware calibration, and uncertainty-aware maintenance decision-making. What remains insufficiently established is a RUL-specific method that jointly adapts calibration to operating conditions and downstream sequential maintenance consequences while preserving reliable uncertainty coverage. Our study will therefore investigate whether decision-aware, condition-adaptive conformal calibration can improve maintenance utility without sacrificing subgroup reliability or merely becoming more conservative.
\end{quote}

\section*{Formal Research Questions and Hypotheses}

\subsection*{Research Question 1: Sequential Maintenance Utility and Coverage Reliability}
\begin{description}[style=multiline,leftmargin=3.5cm]
  \item[Status:] Locked
  \item[Experiment:] \textbf{Experiment A}
  \item[Question:] Can a decision-aware, condition-adaptive conformal RUL calibration method improve sequential maintenance utility while preserving reliable prediction-interval coverage?
  \item[Null ($H_{0,1}$):] The proposed method does not significantly improve maintenance utility over standard global or condition-aware calibration while maintaining comparable coverage.
  \item[Alt ($H_{1,1}$):] The proposed method improves maintenance utility over standard global and condition-aware calibration while maintaining the required coverage reliability.
  \item[Primary Outcome:] Total simulated maintenance cost per engine
  \item[Supporting Outcomes:] Late/failure intervention rate; required prediction-interval coverage
  \item[Comparison:] Proposed method vs.\ standard global conformal calibration and standard condition-aware conformal calibration.
  \item[Threshold Status:] Numerical practical-effect threshold: Deferred until after maintenance simulator validation and before confirmatory testing.
  \item[Supported Logic:] Practically meaningful reduction in maintenance cost per engine supported by 95\% engine-level CI while maintaining required coverage reliability.
  \item[Unsupported Logic:] No practically meaningful cost improvement over strongest baseline, baseline performs better, or lower cost is achieved by violating coverage constraints.
  \item[Inconclusive Logic:] Estimated improvement is favorable but 95\% CI overlaps future threshold, or comparator evidence is mixed.
\end{description}

\subsection*{Research Question 2: Conditional Reliability and Interval Sharpness}
\begin{description}[style=multiline,leftmargin=3.5cm]
  \item[Status:] Locked
  \item[Experiment:] \textbf{Experiment B}
  \item[Question:] Can the proposed method improve reliability across operating regimes and degradation conditions without excessively widening prediction intervals?
  \item[Null ($H_{0,2}$):] The proposed method does not improve worst-group or conditional coverage, or any improvement is primarily obtained through substantially wider intervals.
  \item[Alt ($H_{1,2}$):] The proposed method improves worst-group or conditional coverage while maintaining practically useful interval sharpness.
  \item[Primary Outcome:] Worst-group conditional coverage error
  \item[Supporting Outcomes:] Mean Prediction Interval Width (MPIW); interval score
  \item[Comparison:] Reliability and sharpness of proposed method vs.\ relevant global and condition-aware calibration baselines.
  \item[Threshold Status:] Numerical practical-effect threshold: Deferred until after maintenance simulator validation and before confirmatory testing.
  \item[Supported Logic:] Practically meaningful improvement in worst-group coverage error supported by 95\% engine-level CI without materially degrading interval sharpness.
  \item[Unsupported Logic:] No practically meaningful worst-group reliability improvement, worst-group reliability degrades, or coverage gain requires materially wider/poorer-quality intervals.
  \item[Inconclusive Logic:] Worst-group reliability appears to improve but 95\% CI overlaps future threshold, or reliability-vs-sharpness trade-off is ambiguous.
\end{description}

\subsection*{Research Question 3: Decision-Value Attribution vs. Matched-Conservatism Baselines}
\begin{description}[style=multiline,leftmargin=3.5cm]
  \item[Status:] Locked
  \item[Experiment:] \textbf{Experiment C}
  \item[Question:] Are the maintenance benefits of the proposed method attributable to decision-aware uncertainty calibration rather than simply more conservative prediction intervals or earlier maintenance?
  \item[Null ($H_{0,3}$):] Any maintenance improvement can be matched by simpler conservative baselines such as widened intervals or safety margins.
  \item[Alt ($H_{1,3}$):] The proposed method provides maintenance improvements beyond those achievable through matched-conservatism baselines, demonstrating useful decision information rather than simple over-conservatism.
  \item[Primary Outcome:] Maintenance cost/utility improvement over strongest pre-specified matched-conservatism baseline
  \item[Supporting Outcomes:] Wasted Remaining Useful Life ($RUL_{\text{wasted}}$); premature intervention rate
  \item[Comparison:] Proposed method vs.\ matched-width widened global intervals, fixed safety-margin heuristic policies, and conservative point-prediction rules.
  \item[Threshold Status:] Numerical practical-effect threshold: Deferred until after maintenance simulator validation and before confirmatory testing.
  \item[Supported Logic:] Practically meaningful improvement in maintenance utility over strongest pre-specified matched-conservatism baseline, supported by 95\% engine-level CI.
  \item[Unsupported Logic:] Matched conservative controls reproduce or outperform proposed method's benefit, demonstrating that decision-aware calibration is unnecessary.
  \item[Inconclusive Logic:] Proposed method appears superior but 95\% CI overlaps future threshold, or matched controls yield mixed evidence.
\end{description}

\subsection*{Research Question 4: Robustness and Generalization}
\begin{description}[style=multiline,leftmargin=3.5cm]
  \item[Status:] Locked
  \item[Experiment:] \textbf{Experiment D}
  \item[Question:] Does the proposed method retain its calibration and maintenance-decision benefits across operating conditions, base RUL predictors, datasets, and repeated experimental runs?
  \item[Null ($H_{0,4}$):] The observed benefits are specific to particular datasets, operating conditions, model families, or random seeds.
  \item[Alt ($H_{1,4}$):] The principal reliability and maintenance-utility improvements persist across multiple operating conditions, model families, datasets, and repeated experimental runs.
  \item[Primary Outcome:] Cross-configuration consistency of the proposed method's effect
  \item[Supporting Outcomes:] Effect size; engine-level confidence intervals
  \item[Comparison:] Proposed-vs-baseline performance across predefined datasets/operating conditions, model families, and random seeds.
  \item[Threshold Status:] Numerical practical-effect threshold: Deferred until after maintenance simulator validation and before confirmatory testing.
  \item[Supported Logic:] Consistently favorable and practically meaningful effect across predefined robustness configurations without systematic reversal across datasets, model families, or seeds. (Support does not require positive effect in literally every individual configuration).
  \item[Unsupported Logic:] Benefit disappears, systematically reverses, or is restricted to specific datasets, model families, or seeds.
  \item[Inconclusive Logic:] Mixed results across robustness configurations or statistical uncertainty is too large to confirm robustness.
\end{description}
