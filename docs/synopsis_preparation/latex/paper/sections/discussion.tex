\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Methodological Implications for Turbofan Prognostics}
The core inquiry of the IntelliTwin framework is whether distribution-free uncertainty quantification provides measurable, operational value to industrial decision-makers beyond standard point prognostics or ad-hoc safety margins. Traditional prognostic paradigms focus almost exclusively on minimizing cycle-level error metrics (e.g., RMSE or asymmetric scoring functions). However, in mission-critical applications such as aviation gas turbine maintenance, cycle-level accuracy does not translate directly into operational cost efficiency. A maintenance policy driven by an uncalibrated point predictor is structurally vulnerable to overestimation errors, where an engine is allowed to operate beyond its physical failure boundary, incurring catastrophic unplanned replacement penalties.

IntelliTwin uses an engine-weighted conformal construction motivated by hierarchical conformal prediction. Formal coverage results require the hierarchical sampling and exchangeability assumptions stated in the underlying theory. Equal engine weighting does not establish these assumptions for ordered, lifetime-stratified degradation trajectories. This study therefore evaluates marginal and subgroup coverage empirically. It does not claim guaranteed coverage for each engine, lifecycle stage, operating regime, complete trajectory, or adaptively selected maintenance time. Nominal 90% coverage is a target, not a guaranteed realized coverage proportion. Maintenance outcomes are evaluated separately under the frozen simulator.

\subsection{Conservatism, Asymmetric Penalties, and Negative $\beta$}
A critical methodological focus of this work is the formalization of asymmetric, decision-aware conformal calibration. In maintenance scheduling, the economic structure reflects synthetic preferences under the primary reference scenario ($\CPM = 1.0~\SCU, \CFAIL = 5.0~\SCU, \CWASTE = 0.20~\SCU/\text{cycle}$).

The scaling functions are defined by:
\begin{align}
\sigma(p) &= \left(1 + \frac{p}{125}\right)^\gamma, \quad z(p) = \exp\left(-\frac{p}{20}\right) \\
a(p) &= \sigma(p)\exp(\beta z(p)), \quad b(p) = \sigma(p)\exp(-\beta z(p))
\end{align}
At fixed $p$, $q$ and $\gamma$, negative $\beta$ decreases $a(p)$ and increases $b(p)$. The lower and upper endpoints are therefore nondecreasing relative to $\beta=0$. This does not establish earlier intervention or better maintenance utility because calibration thresholds are refitted and policy outcomes depend on the resulting trajectories.

\subsection{Regime Stratification and Subgroup Reliability}
In complex operational environments such as C-MAPSS FD002 and FD004, turbofans traverse six distinct operating flight regimes characterized by substantial variations in altitude, Mach number, and throttle resolver angle. Global conformal calibration aggregates residuals across all regimes, which can mask severe local undercoverage in rare or highly stressed operating conditions.

Regime-stratified HCP explicitly conditions nonconformity scores on the active flight regime cluster $g \in \{C_1, \dots, C_6\}$. Regime support requires at least $m_g \ge \max(10, \lceil 1/\alpha \rceil - 1)$ distinct contributing calibration engines in that regime (10 engines for 80\% and 90\% coverage; 19 engines for 95\% coverage). When a regime contains fewer than this threshold, the framework executes a deterministic fallback to the pooled global nonconformity quantile for the same method and coverage level, recording fallback.

\subsection{Interpreting Potential Hypothesis Outcomes}
The prespecified statistical protocol explicitly defines decision rules for three potential outcomes across all four research questions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Supported}: All required component conditions pass at the 98.75\% decision interval level, demonstrating statistically significant and practically meaningful superiority.
    \item \textbf{Inconclusive}: No condition fails, but at least one decision interval spans the practical threshold boundary, or inference is degenerate. In this case, no definitive superiority claim is made, and the result is cataloged as requiring expanded engine sample sizes.
    \item \textbf{Unsupported}: Any required component condition fails (e.g., upper CI below margin, lower CI above ceiling, or fleet failure rate increases). This outcome indicates that the prespecified criteria were not supported under the specified experiment.
\end{itemize}
By enforcing strict prespecified boundaries and reporting all comparisons transparently, the research framework ensures that conclusions are dictated entirely by empirical evidence. Final empirical conclusions remain pending held-out evaluation.
