\subsection*{Synthetic Economic Cost Scenarios}
Maintenance decision utility is evaluated across three pre-registered synthetic economic cost scenarios:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Primary Reference} (\texttt{primary\_reference}): $\CPM = 1.0$, $\CFAIL = 5.0$, $\CWASTE = 0.20$ Simulated Cost Units (SCU) per engine.
    \item \textbf{Failure-Sensitive} (\texttt{failure\_sensitive}): $\CPM = 1.0$, $\CFAIL = 10.0$, $\CWASTE = 0.20$ SCU.
    \item \textbf{Waste-Sensitive} (\texttt{waste\_sensitive}): $\CPM = 1.0$, $\CFAIL = 5.0$, $\CWASTE = 0.40$ SCU.
\end{enumerate}
Here $\CPM$ and $\CFAIL$ represent synthetic cost units incurred per overhaul or failure event, and $\CWASTE$ represents synthetic cost incurred per unused operational cycle.

The economic indifference values $R_{\text{equal}} = (\CFAIL - \CPM)/\CWASTE$ are 20 cycles for \texttt{primary\_reference}, 45 cycles for \texttt{failure\_sensitive}, and 10 cycles for \texttt{waste\_sensitive}. At these cycle margins, the cost of preventive intervention plus accumulated discarded life exactly equals catastrophic failure penalty. These are economic benchmark parameters, not policy threshold triggers.

\subsection*{Sequential Maintenance Policy Protocol}
The sequential decision policy operates strictly on observable telemetry states at discrete cycle intervals:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Decision Window}: Actions occur at cycles $t \in [30, T - 1]$. No proactive maintenance action occurs at terminal cycle $T$; if an engine reaches cycle $T$ without prior intervention, catastrophic functional failure occurs.
    \item \textbf{Decision Rule}:
    \begin{equation}
    a_t = \begin{cases} \text{MAINTAIN\_NOW} & \text{if } \LowerBound(t) \le 1.0 \text{ cycle}, \\ \text{CONTINUE} & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Immediate Intervention}: Preventive maintenance occurs immediately upon trigger at cycle $t$, concluding the operational episode without logistical lead-time delay.
    \item \textbf{Cost Realization}:
    \begin{equation}
    \text{Cost} = \begin{cases} \CPM + \CWASTE \cdot (T - t) & \text{if maintenance triggered at } t < T, \\ \CFAIL & \text{if no intervention before cycle } T \text{ (failure).} \end{cases}
    \end{equation}
\end{itemize}
Offline evaluation of prediction intervals spans all cycles $t \in [30, T]$ independently of simulated maintenance policy stopping.
