\section{Proposed Methodology}

\subsection{Preprocessing and Predictive Feature Space}
Evaluations span C-MAPSS FD001 (100 engines), FD002 (260 engines), and FD004 (249 engines). Splits are constructed strictly at the engine level: approx.\ 50\% training, 20\% calibration, 10\% validation, and 20\% held-out testing (Table~\ref{tab:splits}).

Preprocessing uses training-only z-score normalization. Means ($\mu_{\text{train}}$) and population standard deviations ($\sigma_{\text{train}}$) are estimated with degrees of freedom $\text{ddof}=0$. If a training standard deviation is exactly zero, it is replaced by an effective scale of $1.0$, while the mean is still subtracted:
\begin{equation}
x_{\text{normalized}} = \frac{x - \mu_{\text{train}}}{s_{\text{train}}}, \quad s_{\text{train}} = \begin{cases} \sigma_{\text{train}} & \text{if } \sigma_{\text{train}} > 0, \\ 1.0 & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}
\end{equation}
For FD001, global normalization is fitted on training engines. For FD002 and FD004, operational settings are standardized, six operating regimes are learned via $K$-means ($k=6, \text{random\_state}=2026, \text{n\_init}=10$) with lexicographic centroid ordering (\texttt{C1}--\texttt{C6}) and lower-index tie-breaking, and sensor normalization is fitted separately within training regimes. Preprocessing precedes window construction. The canonical 17-feature order is \texttt{setting1, setting2, setting3, s2, s3, s4, s7, s8, s9, s11, s12, s13, s14, s15, s17, s20, s21}.

Causal sliding windows of length $w = 30$ cycles with unit stride ($s = 1$) and no padding are extracted. GBR models use \texttt{sklearn.ensemble.HistGradientBoostingRegressor} on flattened 510-dimensional vectors; LSTM models use a single-layer unidirectional network ($\text{input}=17, \text{hidden}=64, \text{dropout}=0.20, \text{Linear}(64,1)$). Training targets use an upper cap of 125 cycles as a modeling convention. Point predictions are evaluated as $p = \max(0, \text{raw\_prediction})$.

\subsection{Hierarchical Engine-Weighted Conformal Calibration}
To account for unequal engine trajectory lengths, the final hierarchical calibration distribution across $m$ calibration engines satisfies:
\begin{equation}
F_{\text{final}}(q) = \frac{1}{m + 1} \sum_{i=1}^m \left[ \frac{1}{n_i} \sum_{t=1}^{n_i} \mathbf{1}_{\{\text{score}_{it} \le q\}} \right].
\end{equation}
Each finite endpoint carries mass $\frac{1}{(m+1)n_i}$. Total finite mass is $\frac{m}{m+1}$, and remaining mass $\frac{1}{m+1}$ is placed at $+\infty$. For regime calibration, required engine support is $m_g \ge \max(10, \lceil 1/\alpha \rceil - 1)$ (10 engines for 80\% and 90\%, 19 engines for 95\%); otherwise execution falls back to the global threshold.

\subsection{Decision-Aware Asymmetric Nonconformity Score}
The proposed method (\texttt{decision\_aware\_hcp}) modulates scaling and asymmetry as a function of non-negative point prediction $p$:
\begin{align}
\sigma(p) &= \left(1 + \frac{p}{125}\right)^\gamma, \quad z(p) = \exp\left(-\frac{p}{20}\right) \\
a(p) &= \sigma(p)\exp(\beta z(p)), \quad b(p) = \sigma(p)\exp(-\beta z(p))
\end{align}
where $\gamma \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ and $\beta \in \{-\ln 2, 0, +\ln 2\}$. Nonconformity scores and inverted intervals are:
\begin{align}
\text{score}(p, y) &= \max\left(\frac{p - y}{a(p)}, \; \frac{y - p}{b(p)}, \; 0\right) \\
L &= \max\left(0, \; p - q_g \cdot a(p)\right), \quad U = p + q_g \cdot b(p).
\end{align}
At fixed $p, q, \gamma$, negative $\beta$ decreases $a(p)$ relative to $\beta=0$ and increases $b(p)$. The lower bound is nondecreasing and the upper bound is nondecreasing. This does not establish improved maintenance utility or earlier intervention; thresholds are fitted separately and utility must be evaluated. Validation candidate selection froze configuration \texttt{gamma2\_betam1} ($\gamma=2, \beta=-\ln 2$) across all 6 cells under ADR-021.

\subsection{Sequential Maintenance Simulator}
A discrete-event simulation engine replays engine cycles from $t=30$ to $T-1$, executing the pre-registered policy:
\begin{equation}
a_t = \begin{cases} \text{MAINTAIN\_NOW}, & \text{if } L(t) \le 1.0 \text{ cycle}, \\ \text{CONTINUE}, & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Preventive maintenance occurs immediately upon trigger, terminating the episode. Under the \texttt{primary\_reference} scenario, preventive replacement incurs $\CPM = 1.0~\text{SCU}$, unplanned in-service failure at cycle $T$ incurs $\CFAIL = 5.0~\text{SCU}$, and wasted life incurs $\CWASTE = 0.20~\text{SCU}$ per unused cycle. Sensitivity scenarios evaluate $\CFAIL = 10.0$ and $\CWASTE = 0.40$.
