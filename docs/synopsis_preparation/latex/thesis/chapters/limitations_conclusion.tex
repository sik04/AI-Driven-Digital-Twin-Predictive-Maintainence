\chapter{Limitations, Threats to Validity, and Conclusion}
\label{chap:limitations_conclusion}

\section{Threats to Validity and Research Boundaries}
To maintain rigorous scientific standards, the methodological conclusions and empirical findings of this research must be interpreted within explicitly defined boundaries. We identify eight primary threats to validity:

\subsection{Benchmark Simulation Fidelity}
The experimental investigation is conducted exclusively on the synthetic NASA C-MAPSS turbofan engine degradation simulation suite \cite{saxena2008damage}. While C-MAPSS is the preeminent benchmark in PHM research, its degradation curves exhibit strictly monotonic wear profiles. Operational aircraft turbofan engines encounter complex operational realities—such as component washing, partial maintenance servicing, sensor drift, and intermittent fault reversibility—that are not modeled within C-MAPSS.

\subsection{Synthetic Cost and Economic Assumptions}
The discrete-event maintenance simulation operates on study-defined cost preferences under the primary reference scenario ($\CPM = 1.0~\SCU, \CFAIL = 5.0~\SCU, \CWASTE = 0.20~\SCU/\text{cycle}$). Although these parameters realistically reflect the order-of-magnitude cost disparity between scheduled overhauls and in-service engine failures, they represent benchmark simulation preferences rather than empirical airline accounting constants.

\subsection{Finite Fleet Engine Counts}
Across the complete C-MAPSS benchmark suite, 609 engines are evaluated, with 122 engines allocated to the held-out test splits. While this sample size is substantial for academic prognostics benchmarks, statistical power in paired cluster-bootstrap inference across rare flight regimes remains limited.

\subsection{Temporal Autocorrelation, Conformal Exchangeability, and Coverage Bounds}
IntelliTwin uses an engine-weighted conformal construction motivated by hierarchical conformal prediction. Formal coverage results require the hierarchical sampling and exchangeability assumptions stated in the underlying theory. Equal engine weighting does not establish these assumptions for ordered, lifetime-stratified degradation trajectories. This study therefore evaluates marginal and subgroup coverage empirically. It does not claim guaranteed coverage for each engine, lifecycle stage, operating regime, complete trajectory, or adaptively selected maintenance time. Nominal 90% coverage is a target, not a guaranteed realized coverage proportion. Maintenance outcomes are evaluated separately under the frozen simulator.

\subsection{Capped Training Targets vs. Uncapped Evaluation}
Base models are trained using piecewise-capped RUL targets ($R_{\text{max}} = 125$ cycles) as a modeling convention. In contrast, calibration, validation, and held-out test splits evaluate uncapped physical RUL. This distributional discrepancy must be accounted for during early-lifecycle inference.

\subsection{Rare-Group Support and Fallback Protocols}
In complex multi-regime environments (FD002 and FD004), rare flight conditions may contain fewer than the required support threshold $m_g = \max(10, \lceil 1/\alpha \rceil - 1)$ distinct contributing calibration engines (10 engines for 80\% and 90\% coverage; 19 engines for 95\% coverage). When a regime lacks sufficient support, the framework executes a deterministic fallback to the pooled global quantile $q^*_{\text{global}}$ for the same method and coverage level, recording fallback.

\subsection{Research Prototype Boundaries}
The system is an offline, trace-driven prognostic decision-support research prototype; physical engine integration and operational airline validation have not been demonstrated.

\section{Summary of Thesis Contributions}
This project report / thesis draft has established a comprehensive, scientifically audited research framework for industrial turbofan prognostic maintenance:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Methodological Synthesis}: Integration of modern base degradation predictors (GBR, LSTM) with hierarchical, condition-adaptive, and asymmetric conformal calibration.
    \item \textbf{Rigorous Governance}: Complete engine-level disjoint data partitioning, eliminating temporal data leakage across 609 engine units.
    \item \textbf{Principled Baseline Controls}: Formal evaluation against four named control strategies across three distinct families, isolating the true operational value of calibrated uncertainty.
    \item \textbf{Prespecified Statistical Protocols}: Nominal family-wise error rate control via Bonferroni-corrected $98.75\%$ confidence intervals generated through $10{,}000$ paired engine-cluster bootstrap resamples.
\end{enumerate}

Development validation and audit results do not establish confirmatory maintenance benefit, subgroup reliability, or robustness.
