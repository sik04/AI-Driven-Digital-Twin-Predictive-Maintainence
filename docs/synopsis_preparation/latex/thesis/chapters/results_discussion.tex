\section{Methodological Discussion}
\subsection{Conservatism, Asymmetric Penalties, and Negative $\beta$}
The asymmetry parameter $\beta = -\ln 2 \approx -0.6931$ modulates the nonconformity scaling factors $a(p_t) = \exp(-\beta \cdot p_t)$ and $b(p_t) = \exp(\beta \cdot p_t)$. For negative $\beta$, as estimated failure probability $p_t$ increases, $a(p_t)$ decreases below $1$, contracting the lower distance and shifting the unclipped lower bound $L_t$ upward. 

However, as established in the project's statistical protocol, negative $\beta$ does not mathematically guarantee earlier maintenance across all operational trajectories. Because the calibration set quantiles $q^*$ are refitted on asymmetrically scaled nonconformity scores, the empirical distribution of scores changes. Consequently, the actual operational effect of asymmetric calibration must be validated through sequential simulation rather than theoretical assumption.

\subsection{Interpreting Potential Hypothesis Outcomes}
The pre-registered protocol explicitly pre-commits to objective interpretation:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Supported}: Lower confidence bounds strictly exceed practical-effect margins, confirming that conformal calibration provides operational value beyond heuristic baselines.
    \item \textbf{Inconclusive}: Statistical power is insufficient to resolve the practical margin, cataloging the result as requiring larger fleet sample sizes.
    \item \textbf{Unsupported}: Practical thresholds are breached or safety constraints violated, directly falsifying the hypothesis and concluding that simpler baselines suffice.
\end{itemize}
