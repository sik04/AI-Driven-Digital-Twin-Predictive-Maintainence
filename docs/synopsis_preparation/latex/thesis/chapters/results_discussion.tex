\section{Methodological Discussion}
\subsection{Conservatism, Asymmetric Penalties, and Negative $\beta$}
The scaling functions are defined by:
\begin{align}
\sigma(p) &= \left(1 + \frac{p}{125}\right)^\gamma, \quad z(p) = \exp\left(-\frac{p}{20}\right) \\
a(p) &= \sigma(p)\exp(\beta z(p)), \quad b(p) = \sigma(p)\exp(-\beta z(p))
\end{align}
At fixed $p$, $q$, and $\gamma$, negative $\beta$ decreases $a(p)$ relative to $\beta=0$ and increases $b(p)$. The lower bound $L = \max(0, p - q_g a(p))$ is nondecreasing and the upper bound $U = p + q_g b(p)$ is nondecreasing. We do not claim $a < 1$ or $b > 1$ for every $p$, as the $\sigma(p)$ factor also influences their magnitude. Furthermore, this mathematical fact does not establish improved maintenance utility or earlier intervention; nonconformity thresholds $q^*$ are fitted separately and utility must be evaluated empirically in sequential simulation.

\subsection{Interpreting Potential Hypothesis Outcomes}
The pre-registered protocol explicitly pre-commits to objective interpretation:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Supported}: All required component conditions pass at the 98.75\% decision interval level, demonstrating statistically significant and practically meaningful superiority.
    \item \textbf{Inconclusive}: No condition fails, but at least one decision interval spans the practical threshold boundary, or inference is degenerate. In this case, no definitive superiority claim is made, and the result is cataloged as requiring expanded engine sample sizes.
    \item \textbf{Unsupported}: Any required component condition fails (e.g., upper CI below margin, lower CI above ceiling, or fleet failure rate increases). This outcome indicates that the pre-registered criteria were not supported under the specified experiment.
\end{itemize}
Final empirical conclusions remain pending held-out evaluation.
