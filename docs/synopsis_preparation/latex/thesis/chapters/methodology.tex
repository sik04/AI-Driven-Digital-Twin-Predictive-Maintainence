\section{Methodological Architecture Overview}
The IntelliTwin methodological framework links machine learning degradation modeling, distribution-free uncertainty calibration, and sequential decision simulation into a cohesive prognostic pipeline. The pipeline operates under three immutable architectural principles:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Causal Temporal Isolation}: Predictions condition strictly on past operational history within a 30-cycle causal horizon; zero-padding and future lookahead are strictly precluded.
    \item \textbf{Engine-Level Disjoint Calibration}: Conformal nonconformity scores are computed exclusively on dedicated, reserved calibration engine trajectories that share no engine identities with training or held-out test splits.
    \item \textbf{Asymmetric Cost Alignment}: Nonconformity metrics modulate intervals to reflect the severe cost penalties of component failure relative to preventive replacement.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Data Preprocessing and Causal Windowing}
\subsection{Training-Only Z-Score Normalization}
Preprocessing uses training-only z-score normalization. Means and population standard deviations are estimated with \texttt{ddof=0}. An exactly zero standard deviation is replaced by an effective scale of 1.0, while the mean is still subtracted:
\begin{equation}
x_{\text{normalized}} = \frac{x - \mu_{\text{train}}}{s_{\text{train}}}
\end{equation}
where $s_{\text{train}} = \sigma_{\text{train}}$ when $\sigma_{\text{train}} > 0$, and $s_{\text{train}} = 1.0$ otherwise.

Dataset-specific processing executes strictly prior to window construction:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{FD001}: Single nominal operating condition; global training-fitted z-score normalization across all qualified features.
    \item \textbf{FD002 and FD004}: Operational flight settings are standardized using training data; six operating regimes ($C_1$--$C_6$) are learned from standardized training settings using $K$-Means clustering ($k=6$, $\text{random\_state}=2026$, $\text{n\_init}=10$). Canonical regime labels are assigned based on lexicographic centroid ordering. Sensor normalization parameters are fitted separately within each training regime.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Feature Space Construction}
The pipeline enforces a frozen 17-feature selection supported by the recorded qualification procedure:
\begin{center}
\texttt{setting1, setting2, setting3, s2, s3, s4, s7, s8, s9, s11, s12, s13, s14, s15, s17, s20, s21}
\end{center}
Seven sensors ($s_1, s_5, s_6, s_{10}, s_{16}, s_{18}, s_{19}$) are excluded under this recorded qualification procedure; we do not claim they are mathematically constant or universally uninformative across all datasets and regimes.

\subsection{Causal Windowing Protocol}
Temporal feature matrices are formed via rolling causal windows of duration $w = 30$ operational cycles with unit stride ($s = 1$), strictly avoiding zero-padding:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{W}_{i,t} = [x_{i, t-29}, x_{i, t-28}, \dots, x_{i,t}] \in \mathbb{R}^{30 \times 17}, \quad t \ge 30.
\end{equation}
Windows are formed exclusively within continuous engine trajectories; windows never span across engine boundaries, and cycles $t < 30$ are unpredicted. In training splits, target RUL is capped at $\min(\text{rul\_true}, 125)$ cycles as a modeling convention. Point predictions are evaluated as $p = \max(0, \text{raw\_prediction})$ without upper clipping at 125.

\section{Base Degradation Predictors}
We examine two distinct algorithmic paradigms receiving identical 30-cycle causal information horizons:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Gradient Boosted Regressor (GBR)}: \texttt{sklearn.ensemble.HistGradientBoostingRegressor} operating on C-order flattened $30 \times 17$ feature vectors (510 features). Winning configurations selected on validation data across seeds 2026--2027: \texttt{gbr\_lr010\_leaf15\_l2\_0} for FD001, and \texttt{gbr\_lr010\_leaf31\_l2\_0} for FD002 and FD004.
    \item \textbf{Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM)}: Single-layer unidirectional PyTorch LSTM (\texttt{input\_size=17}, \texttt{hidden\_size=64}, \texttt{num\_layers=1}, \texttt{batch\_first=True}, \texttt{bidirectional=False}, internal LSTM \texttt{dropout=0.0}), extracting the final sequence output, followed by \texttt{nn.Dropout(p=0.20)} and \texttt{nn.Linear(64, 1)}. Winning configuration: \texttt{lstm\_h64\_lr0010} (AdamW optimizer, learning rate $\eta = 0.001$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, batch size 256, betas $(0.9, 0.999)$, $\epsilon = 10^{-8}$), using early stopping on validation uncapped-RUL RMSE.
\end{enumerate}
Each selected configuration is trained across five prespecified research seeds (\texttt{2026, 2027, 2028, 2029, 2030}), yielding 30 frozen checkpoints in \texttt{experiments/base\_models/model\_registry.json}. We do not call the five GBR seeds independent experiments, nor assert universal deterministic behavior beyond the recorded runs.

\section{Conformal Calibration Formulation}
\subsection{Engine-Weighted Quantile Computation}
Standard pooled conformal prediction introduces survivor bias in industrial run-to-failure telemetry because long-lived engines contribute more sliding windows than short-lived units.

IntelliTwin uses an engine-weighted conformal construction motivated by hierarchical conformal prediction. Formal coverage results require the hierarchical sampling and exchangeability assumptions stated in the underlying theory. Equal engine weighting does not establish these assumptions for ordered, lifetime-stratified degradation trajectories. This study therefore evaluates marginal and subgroup coverage empirically. It does not claim guaranteed coverage for each engine, lifecycle stage, operating regime, complete trajectory, or adaptively selected maintenance time. Nominal 90% coverage is a target, not a guaranteed realized coverage proportion. Maintenance outcomes are evaluated separately under the frozen simulator.

For FINAL calibration with $m$ contributing engines, where engine $i$ contains $n_i = T_i - 29$ valid endpoints, the empirical distribution function is:
\begin{equation}
F_{\text{final}}(q) = \frac{1}{m + 1} \sum_{i=1}^m \frac{1}{n_i} \sum_{t=1}^{n_i} \mathbb{I}(\text{score}_{it} \le q)
\end{equation}
The mass of each finite endpoint is $\frac{1}{(m+1)n_i}$. The total finite mass is $\frac{m}{m+1}$, with the remaining probability mass $\frac{1}{m+1}$ placed at $+\infty$. The conformal threshold $q^*$ for nominal coverage $1 - \alpha$ is the smallest observed score whose CDF reaches $1 - \alpha$. If no finite score reaches it, $q^* = +\infty$. (In contrast, normalized development empirical CDFs use $1/m$ weighting when selecting configurations via leave-one-engine-out cross-validation).

\subsection{Conformal Calibration Variants}
Let $p \ge 0$ denote non-negative predicted RUL in cycles, $y = \trueRUL \ge 0$ denote uncapped ground-truth RUL, and $g \in \{C_1, \dots, C_6\}$ denote the observed operational regime.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Global HCP (\texttt{global\_hcp})}: Evaluates absolute residual nonconformity $\text{score}(p, y) = |y - p|$. The resulting prediction interval is symmetric prior to lower clipping:
    \begin{equation}
    L = \max(0, \; p - q_{\text{global}}), \quad U = p + q_{\text{global}}
    \end{equation}
    Its displayed width is $p + q_{\text{global}} - \max(0, p - q_{\text{global}})$, which equals $2q_{\text{global}}$ only when $p \ge q_{\text{global}}$.

    \item \textbf{Regime-Stratified HCP (\texttt{regime\_hcp})}: Evaluates absolute residuals, fitting separate regime-specific thresholds $q_g$. Regime support requires $m_g \ge \max(10, \lceil 1/\alpha \rceil - 1)$ distinct contributing calibration engines in that regime (10 engines for 80\% and 90\% coverage; 19 engines for 95\% coverage). When $m_g$ is insufficient, the method falls back deterministically to $q_{\text{global}}$ fitted for the same method and coverage level, recording fallback.

    \item \textbf{Decision-Aware HCP (\texttt{decision\_aware\_hcp})}: Incorporates asymmetric penalty scaling:
    \begin{align}
    \sigma(p) &= \left(1 + \frac{p}{125}\right)^\gamma, \quad z(p) = \exp\left(-\frac{p}{20}\right) \\
    a(p) &= \sigma(p)\exp(\beta z(p)), \quad b(p) = \sigma(p)\exp(-\beta z(p))
    \end{align}
    where $\gamma \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ and $\beta \in \{-\ln 2, 0, +\ln 2\}$. Nonconformity scores and inverted intervals are:
    \begin{equation}
    \text{score}(p, y) = \max\left(\frac{p - y}{a(p)}, \; \frac{y - p}{b(p)}, \; 0\right)
    \end{equation}
    \begin{equation}
    L = \max(0, \; p - q_g \cdot a(p)), \quad U = p + q_g \cdot b(p)
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Mathematical Mechanics of Negative Beta ($\beta < 0$)}
At fixed $p$, $q$ and $\gamma$, negative $\beta$ decreases $a(p)$ and increases $b(p)$. The lower and upper endpoints are therefore nondecreasing relative to $\beta=0$. This does not establish earlier intervention or better maintenance utility because calibration thresholds are refitted and policy outcomes depend on the resulting trajectories.

Candidate \texttt{gamma2\_betam1} ($\gamma=2, \beta=-\ln 2$) was selected as the optimal feasible configuration across all six mandatory cells and frozen under ADR-021.

\section{Discrete-Event Maintenance Simulation}
The sequential maintenance policy executes step-by-step across unseen test engine trajectories. At cycle $t \in [30, T_i - 1]$, if the lower prediction bound satisfies $L(t) \le 1.0$ cycle, the policy issues a \texttt{MAINTAIN\_NOW} command. Preventive maintenance occurs immediately upon decision and terminates the episode (zero intervention lead time).

If maintenance occurs at $t < T_i$, cost is:
\begin{equation}
\Cost = \CPM + \CWASTE \cdot (T_i - t)
\end{equation}
leaving unused operational life $W_i = T_i - t$. If the engine reaches its true physical failure cycle $T_i$ without maintenance, in-service failure occurs, incurring cost $\Cost = \CFAIL$ with wasted life $W_i = 0$.

The study evaluates three synthetic cost scenarios:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{\texttt{primary\_reference}}: $\CPM = 1.0~\SCU, \CFAIL = 5.0~\SCU, \CWASTE = 0.20~\SCU/\text{cycle}$.
    \item \textbf{\texttt{failure\_sensitive}}: $\CPM = 1.0~\SCU, \CFAIL = 10.0~\SCU, \CWASTE = 0.20~\SCU/\text{cycle}$.
    \item \textbf{\texttt{waste\_sensitive}}: $\CPM = 1.0~\SCU, \CFAIL = 5.0~\SCU, \CWASTE = 0.40~\SCU/\text{cycle}$.
\end{itemize}
SCU values represent synthetic benchmark preferences rather than validated airline economics. Offline interval evaluation includes all qualified endpoints $30$ through $T_i$, independently of policy stopping. The equality values (20, 45, and 10 cycles) compare preventive-plus-waste cost with failure cost, and do not represent policy decision thresholds.
