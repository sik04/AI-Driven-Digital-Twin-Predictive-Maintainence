\section{Mathematical Problem Formulation}
Consider a fleet of $N$ turbofan engines indexed by $i \in \{1, \dots, N\}$. Each engine $i$ is monitored over discrete operational cycles $t \in \{1, \dots, T_i\}$, where $T_i \in \mathbb{N}$ denotes the terminal run-to-failure cycle of the unit. At each cycle $t$, an engine emits an operational state vector:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{s}_{i,t} = [u_{i,t,1}, u_{i,t,2}, u_{i,t,3}, s_{i,t,1}, \dots, s_{i,t,21}]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{24},
\end{equation}
consisting of 3 ambient flight condition settings and 21 thermodynamic sensor measurements. Following training-only qualification and variance screening, seven uninformative sensor channels are eliminated, resulting in a standardized $17$-dimensional state vector $\mathbf{x}_{i,t} \in \mathbb{R}^{17}$.

\subsection{Causal History and Information Horizon}
To enforce physical temporal causality and prevent future-information leakage, predictions at cycle $t$ must condition exclusively on a fixed backward temporal window of length $w = 30$ cycles:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{H}_{i,t} = [\mathbf{x}_{i, t-w+1}, \dots, \mathbf{x}_{i,t}] \in \mathbb{R}^{w \times 17}, \quad t \ge w.
\end{equation}
Trajectories are sampled with unit stride ($s=1$) and strict avoidance of zero-padding. Consequently, no prognostic predictions are issued for cycles $t < w$.

\subsection{Target Degradation Variable}
The physical Remaining Useful Life (RUL) of engine $i$ at cycle $t$ is defined as:
\begin{equation}
y_{i,t} = T_i - t, \quad t \in [w, T_i].
\end{equation}
During base model training, an upper piecewise cap $R_{\text{max}} = 125$ cycles is applied to reflect negligible initial degradation:
\begin{equation}
y_{i,t}^{\text{train}} = \min(y_{i,t}, R_{\text{max}}).
\end{equation}
In calibration, validation, and held-out test evaluations, the target remains strictly uncapped ($y_{i,t}$) to evaluate true physical end-of-life accuracy.

\section{Sequential Maintenance Decision Problem}
At each cycle $t \ge w$, the prognostic digital twin generates a point estimate $\hat{y}_{i,t} = f(\mathbf{H}_{i,t})$ and a calibrated lower prediction bound $L_{i,t}(\alpha)$. The maintenance policy $\pi$ issues a binary replacement command $a_{i,t} \in \{0, 1\}$:
\begin{equation}
a_{i,t} = \begin{cases} 
1 & \text{if } L_{i,t}(\alpha) \le \tau_{\text{lead}}, \\
0 & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $\tau_{\text{lead}} \in \mathbb{N}$ denotes the mandatory maintenance planning lead-time.

If replacement is triggered at cycle $t_{\text{maint}} \le T_i - \tau_{\text{lead}}$, scheduled preventive overhaul is executed at cycle $t_{\text{maint}} + \tau_{\text{lead}}$, incurring cost $C_{\text{preventive}}$ and leaving wasted operational life $U_i = T_i - (t_{\text{maint}} + \tau_{\text{lead}})$. If $t$ reaches $T_i$ without scheduled intervention, catastrophic unplanned failure occurs, incurring penalty $C_{\text{unplanned}} \gg C_{\text{preventive}}$ with zero wasted life ($U_i = 0$).

The total simulated maintenance cost for engine $i$ is given by:
\begin{equation}
C_i(\pi) = C_{\text{preventive}} \cdot \mathbf{1}_{\{\text{preventive}\}} + C_{\text{unplanned}} \cdot \mathbf{1}_{\{\text{unplanned}\}} + C_{\text{waste}} \cdot U_i.
\end{equation}
The operational objective is to minimize the fleet-wide mean maintenance cost per engine $\bar{C}(\pi) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N C_i(\pi)$ while strictly bounding the fleet failure rate $P_{\text{fail}}(\pi) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \mathbf{1}_{\{\text{unplanned}, i\}}$.
